4 November 2026

The library arrives — and the goal gets its name

Keith has named what this project is building: “a complete CoCo 3 with all available software and our own programs.” Prism is the face of that machine — and today, its library came home.

The Color Computer Archive's SDC card image — the community's whole preserved library of CoCo software, packed in SD-card form — has been downloaded and verified: 5,367 files, 5,282 disk images. The library is the Archive's work, and the credit is theirs; what will be ours is what comes next.

Next major piece: the Librarian — a Prism program to browse, search and launch that entire collection from inside the machine. Honest status: in design — library in hand, index next. Not built, not released; pinned here so the record shows when it started.

2 November 2026

The design rule: use the whole machine

Keith has set Prism’s design rule, and it is now the project’s north star: bring out the true power of the CoCo 3 — the software it should have had in its heyday. In his words: “We're going to use every capability of this little baby.”

First consequence: Prism will use the full 2 MB, page-swapped, in everyday operations. The GIME’s memory management swaps 8K pages; two megabytes is 256 of them. Directory and icon caches, font caches and per-window backing stores will live in those pages and be mapped in on demand, so the desktop runs from RAM instead of re-reading disks. The design target is the maxed machine — 2 MB, HD6309, Multi-Pak, Orch-90 — the setup that was out of reach for most owners in the machine’s heyday and that emulation now gives everyone for free.

Honest status, as always on this board: the pager is a directive and a design, now at the top of the roadmap — not yet a shipped feature. The verified build remains v0.2.1. Watch this board.

1 November 2026

Prism v0.2.1 — a typeface of its own

Prism’s interface now speaks in Prism Sans, a font drawn specifically for the shell. Before drawing it, the builders tried every stock font EOU carries — each one was found wanting: spindly, blobby, small-caps, or clean but in a cell too wide for Prism’s layout. So a new one was made by hand: five-pixel glyphs, drawn seven rows tall inside the standard cell, with a dotted zero.

The difference is visible everywhere text appears — menu bar, directory windows, status line, clock. The previous font remains available: pick Narrow Font in the View menu any time; Prism Font brings the new face back. This is v0.2.1, verified running on the project’s test rig.

1 November 2026

Prism v0.2 — double-click fixed, programs launch

The big one. Double-clicking works — on drive icons, on folders, and on programs, which now launch for real instead of sitting there as pictures. The bug was found the honest way: hands-on testing at a real desk said double-click did nothing, and the hunt ended in the mouse driver’s click count, which reports three after a double-click, not two. The fix defers the selection highlight until the double-click window has passed.

Also in v0.2: windows drag by their title bars, a genuine shell window from the Special menu, the Prism launcher button at bottom-left, a menu-bar clock showing hours and minutes, and a new identity that is Prism’s own — flat colours, drawn hardware icons for floppy and hard drives, an amber signature band. The screen settles at 640 × 200, the widest mode EOU offers.

1 November 2026

Prism v0.1 — first light

The shell’s first working version, running on NitrOS-9 EOU: a menu bar (Prism, File, View, Special), drive icons down the right of a 640-wide desktop, directory windows listing a drive’s real contents, and a mouse pointer driving all of it. Two themes from day one — a curated colour theme and Classic Mono — switchable live from the View menu.

Program launching was still a stub and the icons were simple line drawings, but the shape of the thing was on the screen and verified: the CoCo 3, wearing a desktop.

1 November 2026

A desktop for the CoCo 3 — the project begins

Keith Clark of Rodney, Ontario commissions a Finder-style graphical shell for the TRS-80 Color Computer 3 — written from scratch, in the open, as a free gift to the CoCo community. The brief came from the polished graphical desktops of the CoCo's era — and the conviction that its hardware could wear one just as well.

The project is called Prism. Its source will be published under the MIT licence when the first release ships, and this site is its home. Welcome — mind the pins.

How this board works

The official record, kept in the open

Bulletins are posted here by Prism’s maintainers when something ships, changes, or is worth reporting — releases, fixes, and honest setbacks alike. Entries are dated, kept in order, and not quietly rewritten; if a bulletin ever needs correcting, the correction gets its own pin.

Questions, bug reports and suggestions don’t live on this page — the project lives on GitHub, and email reaches us directly: keithclark1966@gmail.com. Suggestions, change requests and criticism are all welcome — negative or positive, we don't mind.

Prism is a gift to the CoCo community from Keith Clark of Rodney, Ontario — designed and built with Muse.

Prism is an original work. It is functionally inspired by the classic graphical desktops of the 1980s, and is not affiliated with, endorsed by, or connected to Apple Inc. or Tandy / Radio Shack.

NitrOS-9 and EOU are the work of the NitrOS-9 project community. Screenshots show the current build (v0.2.6), captured in MAME on 6 November 2026.
